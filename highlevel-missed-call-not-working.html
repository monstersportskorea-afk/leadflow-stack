HighLevel Missed Call Text Back Not Working? 7 Things to Check

A missed-call workflow usually fails at one of a few handoff points: the call event, workflow filter, sending number, permissions, or message path.

Short answer

Start with a controlled test call and inspect the workflow history. Confirm the call was classified as incoming and missed, the contact passed every filter, the sending number is configured, and the workflow is published. Then check the message step and any quiet-hour or stop conditions.

Seven checks

1. Confirm the event

Verify the call reached the expected HighLevel location and was recorded as missed, busy, no-answer or voicemail rather than answered.

2. Check workflow status

Make sure the workflow is published and the trigger is attached to the intended account, location and phone number.

3. Review filters

Look for restrictive tags, contact fields, call direction rules or conditions that exclude the test contact.

4. Inspect the number

Confirm the configured number can send the selected message type and that the destination is valid for the account.

5. Read execution history

Use the workflow execution record to identify whether the contact stopped before the message step.

6. Check timing rules

Review wait steps, business hours, quiet hours and stop conditions that may delay or cancel the message.

7. Test the reply path

Send a real test from a permitted number and verify the message, reply handling and human handoff separately.

Step-by-step diagnostic

  1. Call the tracked business number from a test phone and let the call reach the intended missed-call state.
  2. Open the contact and call record, then note the call direction, status, timestamp and location.
  3. Open workflow history and find the execution for that contact and timestamp.
  4. Follow each branch until the first skipped or failed step. Correct only that condition and retest.
  5. After the message arrives, reply to it and confirm that automated follow-up stops or routes to a person.

Common mistakes

  • Testing with an answered call when the trigger only listens for missed calls.
  • Adding a tag filter before the workflow has a reliable step that applies the tag.
  • Changing the workflow but forgetting to publish the latest version.
  • Assuming a delayed message is failed before the wait step has completed.
  • Ignoring local messaging consent, business identification and opt-out requirements.

FAQ

Why does the call appear but not enter the workflow?

Check the trigger's call direction and status criteria, then confirm the call belongs to the same location and number used by the workflow.

Why does the workflow run but no text arrive?

Inspect the message step, sending number, destination formatting, permissions and execution errors. Separate a workflow error from carrier or recipient delivery behavior.

Should I add more automation while troubleshooting?

No. Reduce the test to one trigger, one message and one handoff until the basic path works.
